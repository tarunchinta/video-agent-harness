# A presenter on camera in the film

When the film includes someone filmed on camera (talking-head lines, a world behind them, a view
through their window), plan what stands behind and around them as part of the film from the
start. A presenter cut onto an empty stage, or dropped in as a raw full-frame slate, breaks the
film's world; integrating them later costs a matte, an insert and a re-render per line.

Viewers judge a composite before its content. "It looks green-screened" comes from these, roughly
in order:
- **Plausibility.** Composite only what that camera could see there: a view out of a window at the
  window's height, distance and scale, not an interior or floating graphics at eye level.
- **Light agrees.** The plate's light already says what is outside: a daylit room can't have a
  dark view, and a window can't be darker than the blind or wall it lights. Relight or grade the
  room to the new view, and let bright or coloured CG spill onto the person (a rim or colour wrap
  on hair and shoulders).
- **Match the camera.** Lift CG blacks to the plate's darkest value; match white point, contrast,
  grain, sharpness, compression softness and depth of field; add a glass layer (a faint
  reflection of the room, slight haze) when the CG sits behind glass.
- **Edges.** Segmentation mattes break on hair against bright or busy backgrounds and boil in
  motion, and frame-by-frame flicker shows once the room behind is dimmed; refine mattes
  temporally, keep high-contrast CG away from the hair line, and check edges across a moving
  second, not a still.
- **Pushes.** A 2D push of several times into phone footage blurs the room and flattens the person
  into a cutout. Cut or whip instead, or plan the push when filming and shoot wider.
- **Framing.** When animation must explain behind the presenter, plan the frame before filming. A
  chest-up frame left a band about a fifth of the height above the head, and a mechanism staged
  there failed the mute test twice at phone size. Ask for a wider frame, or show the mechanism as
  a full-frame cutaway while the voice continues.
- **Cut points.** When the presenter's picture follows the VO cut, the VO's pauses are where they
  look at notes. Trim each on-camera shot to eyes on the lens (video-edit's speech-cut rules) and
  let the background or an insert carry the pause.
- **Test first.** Finish one clip at full size and judge it beside an untouched frame of the same
  footage before compositing every insert. Clean hard cuts to the presenter are always available
  when no composite passes.

One measured example: a window replaced by a CG view read as green-screened with CG blacks at
2–4/255 under a plate whose darkest pixel was about 43, and the window darker than the daylit
blind above it. Another composite needs its own measurements.
