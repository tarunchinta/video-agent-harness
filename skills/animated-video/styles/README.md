# Visual styles

A style is the language every frame speaks: how space, light, objects, text and motion are drawn.
Choose it for the brief. The packs below are head starts with lessons attached; a pack saves
research but doesn't decide the film, and a brief may call for a style that has no pack.

| Style | Suits | Signature | Typical failure | Pack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Handmade 2D: paper, ink, held drawings | personal stories, warmth, charm | grain, irregular edges, line boil on twos | a texture filter over clean vectors | [handmade-2d](handmade-2d/README.md) |
| Editorial collage: clippings, cut paper, halftone (Vox, Johnny Harris) | investigations, history, money, politics | cutouts on a paper board, marker circles and arrows, typed labels, 12 fps | a slideshow of clip-art on beige | [editorial-collage](editorial-collage/README.md) |
| Flat infographic, kinetic type, product UI film (Kurzgesagt, Apple, Linear) | data, processes, quotes, software launches | a strict system of type, shape and colour; type that moves with meaning; the real interface | icons in coloured circles; invented UI; motion that makes words harder to read | [motion-graphics](motion-graphics/README.md) |
| Luminous 3D: dark space, light-emitting data, real depth | systems, infrastructure, science, scale | emissive accents, fog, a camera travelling macro to micro | glowing shapes on a flat dark field | [luminous-3d](luminous-3d/README.md) |
| Realistic 3D: a photoreal or physically detailed world | places, machines, nature, anything that must read as real | real scale, materials, sky light, air, weighted motion | reads as CGI: one-plane light, pillow rocks, sliding contacts | [realistic-3d](realistic-3d/README.md) |

A style without a pack gets its bar from the references you look up.

## Choosing for the brief
- Start from the subject's real material: clippings for reporting, the real UI for a product, the
  map for a place. With none, choose by register: warmth, authority, wonder, humour or speed.
- When the subject is a person's own story, ask whether a character or the person on camera
  carries it: in one graphics-only film every judge named "no human on screen" as its ceiling.
- A film reads as one with a lead style; a style borrowed for one beat (because it shows
  something the lead can't) takes the lead's palette and finish.

## Style frames
- Render the **same beat** in each style you offer, at the delivery aspect ratio and finish, each
  that style's honest best; a throwaway scene is enough. Two frames that differ only in lighting
  or palette are one style shown twice.
- Recommend from the brief: a style the user liked on an earlier film is evidence about their
  taste, not a default.
- Feedback that lands between options ("the first, but more realistic") revises the chosen frame
  and keeps what was approved.
