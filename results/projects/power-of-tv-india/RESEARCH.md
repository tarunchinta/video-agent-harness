## The replication, in fuller form

Iversen and Palmer-Jones (3ie Replication Paper 2, 2014; later "All You Need is Cable TV?", Journal of Development Studies, 2019):
- The headline results reproduce with the original data and code.
- Cable shows no effect for women with no schooling.
- Changing how the attitude indices are built weakens the wife-beating and autonomy effects.
- They raise statistical power concerns and flag errors in how school enrollment was constructed.
- Jensen and Oster disputed parts of this. 3ie's own commentary says the replication neither proves nor disproves the original.

This is why the caveat beat stays in, and why the VO says "for some answers" and "the authors think" rather than stating effects as settled.

## Spoken-humanizer pass

- No dashes or ellipses in the VO.
- No "not X, it's Y". The one contrast ("Nobody told her... She just got to watch one") carries two real facts: no explicit message, and exposure.
- No forced threes: beat 2 lists three outcomes because the paper reports those three attitude measures; beat 5's "worked, ran businesses and handled money" is the paper's own list.
- No "studies show": every outside claim names whose it is.
- Contractions throughout.

## Specificity ladder

- Hook: level 3 (a year, a place, a rule you can picture).
- Proof: level 3 (2,700 households, 180 villages, 65 with cable, 2001 to 2003).
- Behavior beat: level 3 (girls' enrollment, birth spacing). Could reach level 4 with an exact effect size from the paper's tables, once the PDF is read.
